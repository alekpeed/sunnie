SUNNIE DAYS: SECURITY, STABILITY, AND FUNCTION REVIEW

Prepared on August 7, 2026

OVERALL VERDICT

This is a strong and careful first draft, but it is not ready for a real user or
the App Store. The ideas and the rule-checking are ahead of the actual proof that
the app works. The safest description is: promising, partly tested, and still in
need of its first full build and hands-on test.

WHAT IS GOOD

1. The app is designed to keep personal information on the owner's devices.
   There are no advertising tools, outside tracking tools, or extra sign-in
   system in the project.

2. No passwords, private signing files, or obvious secret keys were found in the
   saved project files.

3. The app avoids writing journal words, health details, and recordings into its
   activity notes. The notes use general messages and scrambled record labels.

4. Links into the app accept only the app's own kind of link and check that plant
   and trip labels are valid before opening them.

5. The app tries hard not to save the same care action, reward, Watch action, or
   health action twice. Many of the passing checks focus on this.

6. If saved information cannot be opened, the app tries to stay usable and gives
   the user a clear warning that new work will not be kept.

7. The writing rules are kind. The built-in words are checked for shame,
   pressure, unsafe health promises, and misleading food claims.

RELEASE BLOCKERS: FIX THESE BEFORE ANY RELEASE

1. The full app has never been built.

   The iPhone app, Watch app, and home screen displays need a Mac with the current
   Apple app-building tools. The shared decision-making part passed 441 checks,
   but that does not prove the screens, saved information, Watch connection,
   health connection, notifications, sound, or sharing work.

   Why this matters: a project can have excellent written plans and still fail to
   start because of one small mismatch that only Apple's tools can see.

2. Moving old saved information forward has not been proven.

   The project contains eight versions of its saved-information layout, but no
   move from an older version has been run. A mistake here could make a person's
   plants, journal, trips, or wellness history disappear after an update.

   Make several old sample stores, fill them with realistic information, update
   each one, and check every item afterward. Also keep a backup-and-recovery test.

3. The promises about Apple services do not match the default app setup.

   Health sharing and private device-to-device sharing are switched off in the
   default setup because the project does not have the owner's Apple signing
   details. The small home screen displays also cannot receive live information
   until device sharing is turned on. This is understandable for a clean copy of
   the project, but it means those features are not ready.

   The app should either hide these choices until they are truly available or be
   signed and tested with the owner's real setup before release.

4. The owner cannot erase everything from inside the app.

   The privacy plan says the app will let the owner erase one area or all app
   information. The settings screen does not yet provide those controls. This is
   especially important because the app holds journal, wellness, travel, plant,
   photo, and voice information.

   Add a clear review screen, a strong confirmation step, removal of attached
   photos and recordings, and a plain explanation that Apple Health keeps its own
   records separately.

5. Private files need a clear locked-device rule.

   Photos and voice notes are given random names and kept away from ordinary
   backups, which is good. The project does not clearly set or test when those
   files can be opened while the phone is locked. The saved-information store has
   the same unanswered question.

   Choose and test a rule that keeps journals, wellness notes, photos, and voice
   notes unavailable while the phone is locked, without breaking background work.

HIGH-PRIORITY PROBLEMS

1. The plant export leaves temporary copies behind.

   The export guide says temporary copies should be cleaned up. The current
   sharing flow creates a new folder every time and forgets the folder after the
   share screen closes, but it does not remove it. The phone may clean its
   temporary area eventually, but the app should not depend on that for personal
   information.

   Remove each export after sharing finishes or after a short safety delay. Also
   show a friendly error if the export fails; right now failure is silent.

2. Some delete buttons hide failures.

   Several screens ask the app to delete something and ignore any problem. That
   can make the screen look successful even when the item remains. Show a calm
   message and keep the item visible until the removal is confirmed.

3. Journal deletion is really a hidden soft delete.

   This supports a useful undo button, but private journal words may remain in
   storage after the user believes they were deleted. The app needs a stated time
   limit for undo, followed by permanent removal of the entry and its attachments.
   The wording should make the difference clear.

4. The photo permission message is unnecessary and slightly misleading.

   The app uses Apple's private photo chooser, which does not require full photo
   library permission. The project still declares a full-library explanation.
   The permission checker noticed this. Remove the unused explanation unless the
   app later adds direct library access.

5. The camera permission message also appears unused.

   No camera capture was found. Keeping an unused reason can create questions
   during review and makes the privacy story harder to understand. Remove it or
   add and test the promised camera feature.

6. The documents disagree with themselves.

   One section says the shared part passed 441 checks. Other lines still say the
   whole project is uncompiled or that none of 659 checks have run. These mixed
   messages make it hard for a reviewer to know what is true.

   Use one status sentence everywhere: the shared part has passed; the Apple app
   parts and their checks have not run.

7. The automated Apple build has no proven successful run.

   A good automatic check file exists, but the project documents say it has never
   passed. Turn it on, make it pass, and require it before changes are accepted.

MEDIUM-PRIORITY WORK

1. Test every permission by saying yes, saying no, and changing the choice later.
   The app should remain useful in every case.

2. Test with the largest text size, screen reading, high contrast, reduced
   movement, reduced transparency, and color filters. No screen has been seen yet,
   so accessibility is still a promise rather than proof.

3. Test sound with phone calls, alarms, headphones, Bluetooth loss, screen lock,
   other music already playing, and microphone recording.

4. Test the Watch while the phone app is closed, while either device has no
   connection, and when the same action is made on both devices.

5. Test low storage, a damaged saved-information file, interrupted photo saves,
   and an update that stops halfway through.

6. Add limits for very large photos, long lists, very long notes, and years of
   history. The voice recorder already has a five-minute limit, which is a good
   example to follow.

7. Check that every screen explains failures instead of quietly doing nothing.

8. Finish a real privacy page that explains what stays on the phone, what may be
   shared through the owner's Apple account, how to export, and how to erase.

DOCUMENTATION REVIEW

The documents are unusually detailed and often honest about missing proof. That
is a major strength. They explain the product's voice, privacy goals, feature
limits, and testing gaps better than most early projects.

The weakness is repetition. The same status is copied into several files and has
already drifted. Pick one short status page as the only official answer. Other
documents should link to it instead of copying test counts and release claims.

FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Do not add large new features yet. Spend the next round making the existing app
build, protecting private information, proving saved-information updates, adding
erase-all, cleaning exports, and testing on real Apple devices. Once those are
done, the current feature set is already rich enough for a useful first release.
