# Ready-to-paste prompts

Each is self-contained: character, palette, and layout in one block, so you can
paste a single prompt without assembling anything. Attach
`Sunnie_Canon_01_Character_Sheet.png` as a style reference alongside it whenever
the tool allows.

**These are starting points, not a specification.** The composition in each — how
many phones, where the character sits, what is scattered around — is one workable
arrangement, not the required one. Cut it, re-crop it, change the scene, drop the
device mockups entirely. The only part to keep verbatim is the character
description, because that is the part the generator will otherwise get wrong.

## One thing to decide before you start

**Generators cannot spell.** Text inside a generated image comes out garbled or
subtly wrong almost every time, and it is the first thing a person notices.

Two ways through it, and it is worth picking deliberately:

1. **Ask for placeholder text shapes** — soft grey lines standing in for words —
   and lay real type over the top afterwards. This is what a designer would do
   and it is the only route to a headline that is correctly spelled.
2. **Ask for the words anyway** and accept re-rolling until they land. Fine for
   one short word like "Sunnie"; painful for a sentence.

The prompts below take route 1 by default and say so in-line, so you can swap it
if you would rather gamble.

## What to reject

Not a style opinion — composition and scene are yours to choose. These are the
ways a result can be *wrong about the product*, and each is something a generator
produces readily:

- He looks like an adult, a teenager, or a realistic sloth
- A long face, a narrow head, small eyes, or visible shoulders
- Any expression that is sad, cross, disappointed, or smug
- A tab bar with the wrong number of tabs — the app has **five**
- The words "Sunnie Mornings" or "Sunnie Evenings" anywhere
- Streak counters, flame icons, "day 47", or anything implying a broken habit
- Eggs, in any food image
- Harsh outlines, flat vector style, or 3D rendering — this is soft watercolour
