Hello Claude,

You originally built the Sunnie Days project. I asked another reviewer to check
it for safety, privacy, dependability, whether the features really work, and
whether the documents accurately describe its condition.

This message contains the important findings. You do not need a separate review

MAIN CONCLUSION

The project is thoughtful and promising, but it is not ready to release. The
shared decision-making part built on Linux and passed all 441 checks. The full
iPhone app, Watch app, and home screen displays have not been built or opened
with Apple's Mac tools. Their screens, saved information, notifications, Apple
Health connection, Watch connection, sound, and sharing are therefore unproven.

WHAT THE REVIEWER LIKED

1. The app is designed to keep personal information on the owner's devices.
2. No advertising, outside tracking, private signing files, or obvious secret
   keys were found.
3. Activity notes avoid journal words, health details, and recordings.
4. Links into the app accept only the app's own kind of link and check important
   labels before opening them.
5. Many passing checks prevent duplicate care, reward, Watch, and health actions.
6. The built-in writing is checked for shame, pressure, unsafe health promises,
   and misleading food claims.

RELEASE BLOCKERS

1. Prove the complete project builds and opens.

   Build the iPhone app, Watch app, and home screen displays on a Mac. Run every
   available check. Fix what fails. Do not call a feature complete merely because
   its files exist or the shared checks pass.

2. Prove old saved information survives updates.

   The project has eight saved-information versions, but moving real information
   from each older version to the newest one has not been proven. Make realistic,
   filled examples for every old version. Update each one, compare everything
   before and after, and test recovery from an interrupted update.

3. Add honest erase controls.

   The privacy plan promises ways to erase one area and erase everything, but the
   settings screen does not provide them. Permanent deletion must also remove
   related photos and voice recordings. Explain separately that Apple Health
   keeps its own records.

4. Protect private files while the phone is locked.

   Choose and test a clear rule for when the saved store, journal information,
   wellness information, photos, and voice recordings may be opened while the
   phone is locked. Make sure the rule does not break necessary background work.

5. Make Apple features match what the app promises.

   Apple Health sharing, private sharing between the owner's devices, and live
   home screen information are switched off in the clean project copy. Set them
   up and test them with the owner's real Apple signing setup, or hide and clearly
   label them until they are truly available.

HIGH-PRIORITY FIXES

1. Remove temporary plant-export copies after sharing. The current flow creates
   a new temporary folder and forgets it without removing it.

2. Show a calm message when an export or deletion fails. Several places quietly
   ignore the problem, which can make an unsuccessful action look successful.

3. Make journal deletion honest. It currently supports undo by keeping a hidden
   copy. State how long undo lasts, then permanently remove the entry and its
   attachments.

4. Remove the unused full photo-library explanation. The app uses Apple's private
   photo chooser, which does not need full library access.

5. Remove the camera explanation unless camera capture is actually added and
   tested. No camera capture was found during the review.

6. Correct the documents. Some places say the shared 441 checks passed while
   other places still say none of 659 checks ran. Use one official status page.
   The accurate starting statement is:

   “The shared part passed 441 checks. The iPhone, Watch, and home screen parts
   and their checks have not yet been run with Apple's Mac tools.”

REAL-LIFE TESTS STILL NEEDED

1. Refuse every permission and confirm the main app remains useful.
2. Fill the phone nearly full and try saving a photo and voice note.
3. Interrupt saves and confirm the app recovers without losing earlier work.
4. Turn connections off during Watch actions and restore them later.
5. Make the same care action on the phone and Watch at nearly the same time.
6. Interrupt sound with calls, alarms, headphones, and microphone use.
7. Test very large text, screen reading, high contrast, reduced transparency,
   reduced movement, and color filters.
8. Use a real iPhone and paired Watch for behavior that a pretend device cannot
   prove.
